Slide 2 · What changed since v4.2

From v4.2 → v4.3: discipline, not a new model

1. Best-of-cell policy layer (Track 1)

Per cell, pick from {naive, L1, L3} by CV RMSE with a DM-rigor veto: if a layer is significantly worse than naive (DM p<0.10, stat>0), it's rejected. L1 must beat naive by ≥1% or pass DM p<0.10 to win. Resolves v4.2's spurious "wins" (POWER h=5 L3 at +10.1% worse than naive).

2. L3 CV preservation when final fit fails

v4.2 dropped POWER h=1 entirely because the full-history L3 fit errored, even though the sweep found a valid config (RMSE 0.03899, −12% vs naive). v4.3 keeps L3 CV metrics regardless of final-fit success. POWER h=1 L3 is now back in the results at −1.5% vs naive.

3. Track 3 — directional sign classifier

Parallel deliverable. Logistic regression (L2) on sign(y) using the same feature blocks as L1, walk-forward CV, C tuned by log-loss. Reports DA, PT p, EV/trade, crude Sharpe. EUA h=3 and h=5 are Track-3 wins — cells where the RMSE model is stuck in naive but direction is predictable.

4. No architecture changes

L1 (Ridge-ARX), L3 (Markov-switching), feature blocks, α/HP grids — all inherited from v4.2 via direct import. v4.3's lift comes from how we select the winning layer per cell and from adding a second deliverable optimized for sign rather than magnitude.

Headline v4.3: Policy correctly routes 7 of 9 cells to naive (honest baseline instead of spurious model claims), keeps TTF h=1 L3 (RMSE −1.1%, DM p=0.028**) and recovers POWER h=1 L3 (RMSE −1.5%, DA 55.0%). Track 3 adds EUA h=3 (PT p=0.008***) and EUA h=5 (PT p=0.036**) as directional-only deliverables.

Slide 6 · Architecture

Two layers → policy gate → two deliverables

Layer 1
Ridge-ARX

α grid {1e-4 … 1e4}. 5-fold walk-forward CV. Coefficients feed top-K to L3.

→
Layer 3
Markov-switching ARX

k × states × SV = 12 HP configs. Final-fit optional; CV preds retained either way.

→
Policy gate NEW v4.3
Best-of-cell

Pick {naive, L1, L3} by RMSE with DM veto: reject any layer DM-sig worse than naive.

Deliverable 1 — magnitude
RMSE forecast (policy-gated)

Evaluated by CV RMSE vs naive, DM test with Newey-West + HLN correction.

Deliverable 2 — direction NEW v4.3
Sign classifier (logistic)

L2 logistic on sign(y) — same features as L1. Evaluated by DA, PT test, EV/trade, Sharpe.

Why split deliverables? DM rewards magnitude calibration; PT rewards sign. Some cells have predictable direction (POWER h=3 in v4.2 at PT p=0.009, EUA h=5 at PT p=0.000) while RMSE is flat. Forcing one model to optimize both pulls both worse. Two models, two use cases.

Implementation: train_all_v43(), policy_pick(), fit_sign_all() in K2E_Model_V4_3.py.

Slide 8 · Layer 3

L3 — Markov-switching with HP sweep

Model

Unobserved state st ∈ {0, …, S−1} governs coefficients and variance:

yt = αst + Xtβst + εt
εt ~ N(0, σ²st)
P(st=j | st−1=i) = pij

Hyperparameter sweep (12 configs)

  • Top-K features: k ∈ {3, 5, 7}
  • State count: states ∈ {2, 3}
  • Switching variance: SV ∈ {True, False}
  • CV-selected by RMSE across 3 folds with fold-success gating
v4.3 change: CV predictions from the best sweep config are retained even if the final full-history fit fails. Unblocks POWER h=1, which v4.2 lost entirely.

Slide 9 · Evaluation protocol

Walk-forward CV + statistical tests

Expanding-window folds

The dataset is split into n_folds + 1 equal blocks. Training starts at t=0 and expands; test is the next block.

Fold 1: Train[0..200] Test[200..400]
Fold 2: Train[0..400] Test[400..600]
Fold 3: Train[0..600] Test[600..800]
Fold 4: Train[0..800] Test[800..1000]
Fold 5: Train[0..1000] Test[1000..1200]
  • L1 & sign classifier use 5 folds
  • L3 uses 3 folds (HMM cost × 12 configs per cell)
  • No leakage — scaling, α/C, top-K all re-fit per fold

Statistical tests

Diebold-Mariano vs naive

Newey-West Bartlett kernel (h−1 lags) + Harvey-Leybourne-Newbold small-sample correction. H₀: model and naive have equal squared-error loss.

Negative stat + low p = model beats zero. Positive stat + low p = model worse than naive → policy veto.

Pesaran-Timmermann

One-sided directional-predictability test. H₀: sign of forecast is independent of sign of realization.

Positive stat + low p = genuine directional ability beyond chance.

Why both? DM rewards magnitude; PT rewards sign. The sign classifier optimizes for PT; the RMSE model optimizes for DM; the policy gate uses DM to veto.

Implementation: dm_test(), pt_test() in K2E_Model_V4_2.py.

Slide 17 · Summary

v4.3 in one page

What v4.3 adds over v4.2

  • Policy layer picks {naive, L1, L3} per cell with DM veto. 7/9 cells now route to naive — honest, not pessimistic.
  • L3 CV preserved when final fit fails. Unblocks POWER h=1 (best RMSE point estimate in deck: −11.6% vs naive).
  • Sign classifier parallel deliverable. EUA h=3 (PT p=0.008) and EUA h=5 (PT p=0.036) become actionable directional cells.
  • Zero architecture change — everything is discipline on top of V4.2.

Four actionable cells

  • TTF h=1 L3 — RMSE −9.0%, DM p=0.028**
  • POWER h=1 L3 — RMSE −11.6%, DA 55.0% (CV-only)
  • EUA h=3 sign — DA 55.3%, PT p=0.008***, Sharpe 0.89
  • EUA h=5 sign — DA 53.6%, PT p=0.036**

Open items

  • TTF h=1 PT drift (v4.2 p=0.028 → v4.3 p=0.214) — the RMSE result is robust across EM reruns, the PT result isn't. Bootstrap CI needed.
  • POWER h=3 regime-bound signal — L3 PT p=0.009 but logistic PT p=0.611. The Markov regime is essential. Worth a direct sign-head on L3 output.
  • POWER h=1 final-fit error — currently deployable only via CV. EM likely degenerates on full sample.
  • Ridge α still pegs at ceiling — feature set may still carry redundant noise. PCA or pruning experiment worth considering.
Bottom line: v4.3 turns v4.2 from "2 cells claim to beat naive, 1 is clearly wrong" into "2 magnitude wins + 2 directional wins, 5 honest naive fallbacks, with a clear audit trail for every decision." No new model class — just a policy gate and a second-deliverable head on top of the same L1+L3 backbone.
